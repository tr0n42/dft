The Dark Forest Theory · ground units · 26 November 2026

Ground units v2

This is version 2 of the swarm's ground units, as built on 25 and 26 November 2026 from the behaviour we agreed class by class. Each unit has a live demo on a small test planet, then a write-up of what I built, what went wrong and how I fixed it, the numbers it landed on, and the questions I have for you.

How to use it:

  • Scroll to a unit. Its demo starts when it comes into view and pauses when you scroll away, to save battery.
  • The line under each demo says what the unit is doing right now. Replay starts the demo again from the beginning.
  • The demos are drawings of the agreed behaviour, in the mockup's one-metre blocks. They run faster than the game, and a few depths are shortened so everything fits on screen (each demo says where). The game itself runs the real simulation.
  • Give feedback per unit: the "Open questions" list at the end of each one is where I most need your call.

Sapper

small · a 5 kt nuclear charge · made to order where cutters struggle

What it does

A small machine carrying a nuclear charge. When cutters hit rock so hard that they slow right down, the director sends one. It drills deep under the hard ground and goes off, and everything above it turns into loose rubble that cutters can scoop up quickly.

What I built

  • The charge's size (its yield) sets everything: how deep it goes, how much ground comes loose, the damage and the plume. Depth is 46 m times the cube root of the yield in kilotons, so 79 m for today's 5 kt charge.
  • A loosened column: every solid cell from the charge up to the surface becomes loose rubble. It is as wide as the blast at the bottom and 1.6 times that at the top. Hard rock sometimes holds together in big cracked chunks. About 5,000 cells per blast.
  • Made to order instead of a fixed count. A flock counts as struggling when it has cut at least 3 loads lately and each kg takes 3 times as long as in soft rock (hardness 3; granite is about 4 times). Then a sapper is ordered, at most one every 90 seconds and at most 3 out at once.
  • Blasts are dangerous. Cutters in the column (and 4 m round it) are wrecked, and their scrap is kept. Haulers within 3 blast widths of where it breaks the surface are damaged, and destroyed within the first third of that.
  • It never catches a tunneler. If one is too close, it drills sideways (up to 200 m) to get clear, or waits armed.
  • Out of sight, a stalled stretch gets the same loosening done by arithmetic, with no plume, and the share of cutters working there is lost.
  • The plume grows with the yield and is drawn only when it would be at least 20 pixels tall on screen, so a big one shows from far out.

Problems found and how they were fixed

  • On the Dev planet nothing struggled for over 15 minutes, because cutters pick the easiest cells and there are 120 m of soft layers over the granite. That is the right behaviour, so no sapper was needed there.Fixed: a sapper testbed (3 km, 8 m of soft rock over granite and gabbro). There flocks reach 4.1 times slower at about 4 minutes, and a sapper follows every 90 seconds.
  • Sappers went off at the surface. The dust from their own drilling fell back on them and counted as being buried.Fixed: being buried never sets one off now. Only magma or the heat of another blast does.
  • The loosened column came out short (50 m, once only 1 m). It measured the surface by looking straight down, which meant down the sapper's own shaft.Fixed: it now reads the surface on either side of the shaft.
  • Off screen almost no cutters died, because off-screen cutters do a whole trip at once and wait at their hauler.Fixed: a cutter is caught if where it is, where it is going, where it came from or where it last cut is in the column. Off screen now loses 140 to 190 cutters a blast on the testbed, the same as on screen.

Tunneler

the opener · the vanguard · sees through the fog

What it does

The vanguard. A big drilling machine that sees through the fog, picks the ground most worth opening (material valuable to the swarm once it leaves, and what the swarm needs now), and bores a curving shaft down to it. It caps magma so cutters don't die in it, drills through very hard rock for them, and at the very end goes off deep down as a big nuclear blast: a final gift of matter.

What I built

  • A rewrite, "the opener". (The old tunneler stays only for the old perk and old saves.)
  • Value: every 60 seconds it rates each material by its worth in space and by how short the stockpile is of what cutters and haulers are made of.
  • Targets: it looks at columns every 96 m across the swarm's reach and 3 km past it, down to 2 km, checked every 24 m. It can see through the fog to do this. A target's score is its worth divided by (1 + depth ÷ 300 m), so near and needed comes first without forgetting the valuable one. Magma layers and hard rock under struggling flocks are special targets.
  • Steering: it turns at most 0.6° per metre and never goes past 20° off straight down, so its shafts curve.
  • Crew: its own cutters take all of its spoil, with the mass kept whole. It waits if they fall behind.
  • Magma: when magma comes within reach of its drill, it caps it (the magma 4 m past the drill freezes to glass) and leaves a job for a heat-sink bot. Then it backs up its shaft and looks for the next target.
  • End: with nothing left anywhere, it sinks back down its shaft, stopping 80 m short of the bottom, and goes off: 50 kt.

Problems found and how they were fixed

  • With a crew of 30 cutters (3.6 tonnes a second), the drill could do 130 tonnes a second but spent the whole run waiting: 156 m in 15 minutes.Fixed: a crew of 200 (30 tonnes a second). Then the drill became the limit at 10 m a minute in granite, so its power went from 100 to 400 times a cutter's (0.8 m a second in granite), which matches the crew. It now bores about 450 m in 10 minutes.
  • Right after capping magma it found nothing further down (everything was behind the cap) and blew itself up, wasting the unit and risking the cap.Fixed: it backs up to its shaft's mouth and searches its whole reach again for a new shaft. Only when nothing is left anywhere does it go off, well short of the cap.
  • Depth readings taken straight down a shaft found the shaft's bottom instead of the surface.Fixed: a new reading of the surface on either side of the shaft, used by sappers too.

Heat-sink bot

two roles · freezes magma to stone · its own crew

What it does

It cools magma so the swarm can work through it. Role 1: it settles over a small magma pool near the cutters and freezes all of it. Role 2: under a tunneler's cap, it freezes a passage straight down through a magma layer, wide enough for a tunneler, so tunnelers can carry on down. It makes a passage, not the whole layer.

What I built

  • Every 30 seconds it looks for open pools near the flocks. Small ones (up to 1,500 cells, all within its 30 m reach) are pools to cool. Wide ones under open space become deep jobs, 40 m apart, with as many bots as fit.
  • The director builds as many as there is work for, up to a cap, deep jobs first.
  • Freezing: 40 cells a second at the freezing point. A hotter cell costs up to 4 times as much. Heat only sets how long it takes; cooling never fails. No power is beamed up for now.
  • Deep job: it goes down the tunneler's shaft to the cap. Its front freezes a slab across the passage (a tunneler's 12 m plus 3 m of frozen wall each side) a metre at a time. Its crew of 60 cutters digs out only the middle 12 m behind it, so the walls stay. The passage is through when the front has gone 6 m past the cap with no hot liquid and the crew has caught up. Then the tunneler's shaft is extended and tunnelers go down it.
  • End: idle for 10 seconds with no next job, it powers down and is left as scrap for the cutters. Magma reaching it wrecks it.

Problems found and how they were fixed

  • Passages counted as "through" after only 4 to 6 m, because the tunneler's cap had already frozen the first metres.Fixed: clear metres count only past the cap.
  • Passages ran 400 m through a 90 m layer: the front kept going through the solid ground under the layer while it waited for the crew.Fixed: it stops once clear and lets the crew catch up. Passages are now 119 to 135 m.
  • 5 bots were wrecked by magma: slanting passages left gaps in their walls.Fixed: the walls are now laid in two rows a metre, half a metre apart. None wrecked since.
  • A tunneler capped the same breach 17 times, 3 m deeper each time, because the passage wasn't really through.Fixed: a cap within 40 m of a known job reopens that job.
  • On the testbed the lower mantle was 1,700 K, so any rock disturbed there melted, making an endless magma layer.Fixed: the testbed's mantle is now 1,000 K.

Open questions for Benjamin

  • On the lava world (a magma ocean under a thin skin), heat sinks still lose about 8 bots in 20 minutes to magma rising over them while they work, down from 15. Is that "magma doing what magma does", or should they back off when the magma rises?
  • A lava world flooding through a tunneler's hole is expensive to simulate on screen: 1 to 43 ms per game second while you watch it closely, and much more if a whole planet were simulated in detail (only tests do that). Worth a look when you zoom in on one.
  • Is 1,500 cells the right limit for a "small" pool it cools completely?
  • Steam is drawn only when it's cheap at that zoom. Is it showy enough?

Forester

a crab on three legs · claws shear trees · its laser burns the cover

What it does

Mostly for fun. It roams the planet ahead of the cutters, clearing plant life that might be hostile or hard to reach. Its claws shear trees into a clippings bag slung behind it, and its laser sets the ground cover alight. Fire spreads to anything that burns nearby. It has no crew: the ash waits for whatever cutters arrive.

What I built

  • The look is the mockup's: a crab facing forward on three sprawling legs, curved legs with knees riding high, and the stride with its sag, jolt and surge. The game had drifted to a sideways crab on four legs; that version is gone. (A helper did this part and it was merged.)
  • Flight: it is launched over the thickest stand of plants anywhere on the planet and flies folded, a bit like a UFO, past the swarm's edge if need be. Then it drops, unfolds and starts work.
  • Retiring: when walking to the next plants would take longer than building a new one and flying it there (plus 8 seconds), it powers down. Its bag and its body are left for the couriers to take back up.
  • Fire out of sight: what its laser lights where nobody is looking becomes a fire front burned by arithmetic, spreading to the plants around it. In view it becomes real fire. Burn gases are lost to the sky.
  • A full bag is dropped on the ground for the couriers.

Problems found and how they were fixed

  • The game's forester no longer matched the mockup: sideways, four legs, its bag under its middle and its laser firing from the eye stalks.Fixed: redrawn and re-animated from the mockup: forward-facing, three legs, bag behind, laser from the front, the mockup's gait.

Abductor

a spider on three legs · palps take animals alive · its laser bursts what spits or runs

What it does

The forester's twin, for animals. Its palps snatch grazing animals alive into its sac. Its laser bursts animals that spit at the swarm, or that run, into gore and biomass. Its laser never starts fires. It is nearly invulnerable.

What I built

  • Same flight, retiring rule and out-of-sight rule as the forester. It goes to the densest animals, counting those within 200 m.
  • Gore: there are no blood or flesh materials yet, and adding them would change every planet's fingerprint. So a lasered animal's mass is credited as biomass at once, and the game draws a red burst (sparks and red dust).
  • Live catches are now credited at their own mass (the old double value is gone). Cutters don't hunt animals.
  • Spitters: animals with a ranged attack (a green spine). They spit every 6 seconds at machines within 60 m. A walker loses 5 health (an abductor has 100,000); otherwise a flock within reach loses a cutter.
  • Herds out of sight: calm animals nobody sees, more than 400 m from any walker, move every 10 seconds instead of every frame.

Problems found and how they were fixed

  • It lasered every animal that ran and caught nothing alive, because animals panic the moment it arrives.Fixed: spitters first (a shot every 2 seconds), then the palps for anything in reach, and runners out of reach only every 3 seconds. Now about 70 to 90 caught alive and 240 to 290 burst in 15 minutes.

Couriers

a picture of the logistics · cost nothing · can't die

What it does

Couriers are only a picture of the logistics arithmetic. Material really moves as flows with distance delays; the couriers show what that arithmetic is doing. They cost nothing, can't die, and fade back into the mother swarm when done. Their only job on the surface for now is taking up full forester bags and abductor sacs (and the body of a walker that has retired).

What I built

  • A full bag or sac is dropped on the ground. Couriers reach it after 20 seconds plus its distance from the mother swarm at 60 m a second. The load is credited as they lift it.
  • On screen they come down at 40 m a second from 150 m up, latch on for 0.6 seconds (lines of light down to the load), then lift faster and faster and fade into the swarm.
  • A group is 2 couriers plus one for every 7 items: 5 for a forester's bag, 4 for an abductor's sac.
  • A retired walker's bag and body (as scrap, its parts' mass) go up the same way.

Problems found and how they were fixed

  • None logged. This part of the run was a check that couriers match the agreed design, not a rewrite.
