Choices to look at

Biomechanical

The machines look grown as much as built, like the Matrix's machines: rounded carapace with overlapping plates over the frame, exposed ribs rising off their backs, vertebrae where modules stand apart, dark sinew cables sagging in loops, tendrils hanging and swaying, clusters of sensor eyes, horns for smokestacks, hammer legs bent at the knee like an insect's, rounded vats. Ships have domed holds, a head with eyes at the bow and a segmented tail at the stern. Sinew is a cold dark metal, bone a pale one; lights stay the swarm's colour. This holds for every machine from here on.

Nine planets, two pages

Each cell is one planet and a machine built for it: a refinery on the Refineries page, a storage ship on the Storage ships page. Metallic, rocky, volcanic; living, ocean, fungal swamp; ice moon, hothouse, carbon world. Life works and life grafts appear only on the living world, ocean, fungal swamp and hothouse (a little life).

One parts library for every ship

Every feature is a part in one library, tagged with the matter it works best and where it mounts; a machine draws the parts suited to its job, by its planet's weights. Refineries draw processing parts; storage ships draw from parts made for storing (below).

  • Underneath: hammers (metal), crucible pour (metal), rolling mill (metal), electrolysis bank (fluid), centrifuge (fluid), jaw crusher (ore), soil sifter (ore), fractionating column (gas), cryo condenser (gas), log chipper, digester, rendering press, blood still, bone mill (life); hands (life), muscles (metal).
  • On top: horn stacks (ore), globe tanks (gas), crane (metal), ribs, lungs (gas). Storage ships have a library of their own (see above).
  • On the face: vats (fluid), furnaces and ember grilles (ore), eyes (any), heart, bones, ribcage (life), veins (fluid).

The planet shapes its refineries

Every feature is classed by the matter it works best, and a planet's mix decides what its refineries and ships grow. Pick a planet in the panel at left.

  • Metal: hammers, crucible pour, rolling mill, crane; muscles; bar racks and vaults.
  • Ore: jaw crusher, furnaces, smokestacks, soil sifter; jaws; ore cages and hoppers.
  • Fluid: vats, centrifuge, electrolysis bank; veins.
  • Gas: fractionating column, cryo condenser, globe tanks; lungs; tank rows.
  • Life: log chipper, digester, rendering press, blood still, bone mill; bones, ribcage, heart, hands, skull.
  • Any world: eyes.

Extra jobs, works and holds are picked by those weights; a graft appears with a chance set by its class's weight (certain on the world it suits, never where the planet has none of that matter). Every refinery still has a forge, stacks, vats and a furnace. Life works only where there is life.

Grafts (switch each in the panel at left)

Machine twists on body parts, each a separate switch layered on top of the base design, so any can be removed if we go too far toward the biological:

  • Eyes: big mechanical eyes; the pupil lamp tracks the nearest courier; the lids blink.
  • Bones: metal bones with knuckled ends across hull faces or bracing between modules.
  • Ribcage: bone bars caging the molten vats.
  • Heart: a metal heart in a chamber, thumping a double beat.
  • Jaws: mandibles that chomp at the ore intake; a lower jaw under a ship's bow.
  • Muscles: striated slings between modules that contract and relax.
  • Veins: channels in the carapace with pulses of light running along them.
  • Hands: skeletal metal hands hanging underneath, fingers curling and opening.
  • Lungs: bladders on top that breathe.
  • Skull: on ships, a plated cranium over the bow with sockets and teeth.

Life works (only on planets with life)

  • Log chipper (tree trunks): a trunk slides into a toothed drum; chips spray out; pulp bales.
  • Digester (biomass): a sac of sludge churns and bubbles, bleeding off gas; methane canisters and slurry drums.
  • Rendering press (meat): meat on hooks rides the rail into a press; tallow drips into drums.
  • Blood still (blood): a pump throbs pulses of red round a glass coil; a flask fills and separates; blood drums.
  • Bone mill (bones): bones tumble between rollers; white bone meal falls into a bin.
  • Soil sifter (soil, peat, humus): a sieve shakes; soil falls through; stones roll off the end.

They show on the planets with life (Balanced, Living world, Ocean); in the game they would be built only on bodies whose life allows it.

Built from modules

Each refinery is a cluster of hull modules joined edge to edge, with a dark joint line where two meet. Shapes: box, keel, wedge, drum, tower, pod, and bridges where two modules stand apart. Every module is the same armour, struts and panel lines as the other ships; heat is what sets a refinery apart.

Each module has a job

  • Forge: a row of short drop-hammers under it, each on its own beat; every third blow finishes an ingot.
  • Stacks: 2 to 4 smokestacks on top, flared, crowned, cowled or plain.
  • Vats: 1 to 3 glass vats in its face, each its own molten liquid (iron, white-hot, crimson, toxic green, cyan salt, violet).
  • Furnace: furnace mouths and ember grilles.
  • Globes: round glass tanks of liquid standing on top.
  • Crane: a mast and jib with a swaying chain and hook.

Every refinery gets at least a forge, stacks, vats and a furnace; the rest roll. Plates, rivets, a pipe run, chains, spikes, lamps and the ore hopper go on top of that.

Work hung underneath

Besides the drop-hammers, seven works for solids, fluids and gases. Each refinery hangs a few under its modules; all seven appear across the six shown.

  • Crucible pour (molten metal): heats until it glows, tips on its pivots, pours a stream into a mould; the ingot cools from white to metal.
  • Fractionating column (gases): bubbles rise through its window, amber to pale; a canister fills at the bottom; the side taps puff vapour.
  • Centrifuge (fluids): the rotor spins behind a round window; the sight glass settles into layers; it fills drums.
  • Electrolysis bank (fluids to light metals): electrodes dip into a bath under a copper busbar; blue-white arcs crackle; metal plates come out.
  • Jaw crusher (ore): chunks drop down a chute, two jaws chomp, gravel and dust fall into a bin.
  • Rolling mill (hot metal): a glowing slab goes between two rollers and out as a thin sheet; a shear cuts it.
  • Cryo condenser (gases to liquid): a bellows pumps; frost glitters on an icy coil; drips fill a dewar flask; cold mist drifts off.

Products go to the ships' racks in their own shape: bars for solids, banded canisters for gases, ribbed drums for fluids.

Sizes (buttons along the bottom)

  • Refineries: small 3 to 6 modules; medium about 10 to 16, each up to 1.3 times bigger; large about 14 to 19 (1.6 times); super about 27 to 37 (2 times), plus their corridors and hubs. Works, hammers, stacks and grafts come with the modules.
  • Storage ships: small about 60 to 88 m long; medium 100 to 150; large 160 to 230; super 260 to 360. Bigger ones have rows of holds with frame bands between, several windows to a row, a thicker frame, more tractor beams and cupolas.
  • Each window is either one big hold of a single kind of matter or bays split by ribs; the bigger the ship, the more often a big single hold.

Planned layout for bigger refineries

Small refineries still grow free-form. From medium up they are laid out like a town:

  • Spine corridors run the length: one for medium and large, two for super. Corridors are tubes with rails on both sides, bone rings every 6 m, and a glazed walkway down the middle with lamps in it.
  • Cross corridors cut the town into sectors: medium has 2 sectors split by a hub, large 3, super 4. Hubs (a ring of strut and bone round a lit core) sit where corridors meet and at both ends of every spine.
  • Each row of a sector (above the spine, below it, and on super between the two spines) has one job and is built from that job's family of shapes: forges from boxes and keels, furnaces from drums and domes, vats from boxes and drums, stacks from domes with tall horns, and so on. The first four rows are always forge, stacks, vats and furnace.
  • Inside a row the modules still vary in shape and height, so the skyline stays a mini-city.

Bigger storage ships: same shape, better fitted

Every size keeps the same hull shapes (lore: one hull design, scaled, is cheaper to build). What grows with size is the fit-out:

  • Medium and up: bulkheads between the holds in one of five styles: ribbed, cross-braced, riveted, vertebral, or lit double rails. Also antenna arrays on the roof and a row of running lights along the top edge.
  • Large and up: a thicker frame, docking clamps on both ends (bigger jaws on super), vent grilles underneath that glow warm, and eye clusters at the lower corners of the hold frame (part of the Eyes graft, so they switch off with it).
  • Super: short separate ribs standing along the roof (no continuous bar) and panel seams on the hull.

Storage ships: holds you can see into

The main feature is that you can see what a storage ship holds. A boxy hull (box, flattened hexagon, a box with a raised deck, or a lens-topped box) with most of its face one big window, split by bone ribs into bays; each bay holds one kind of matter, picked by the planet from the storage ships' own library:

  • Bar rack (metal): bars stacking up as they come.
  • Ore bay (ore): compressed ore heaped to its level, rising and falling.
  • Canister rack (gas): banded canisters.
  • Liquid tank (fluid): the liquid to its level, with bubbles.
  • Organics rack (life, only where there is life): bales and drums.

A little of the UFO stays: rim lights chasing along the bottom, a tractor beam underneath (lit while couriers load and unload), sometimes a small glass cupola on top. Biomechanical touches are kept quiet: bone ribs between the bays, a sinew seal round the window, a pair of eyes on the frame, a few tendrils on living worlds. A full bay sends its load on.

Material flow

Each column of refineries is served by the storage ship below or above it. Couriers carry raw ore from the ship's cages and hoppers to each refinery's hopper, and cooled ingots back to its bar racks. Refined bars are 3 m by 2 m, packed edge to edge, brighter than the ore they came from, with a bright top edge and a glint sweeping across the hold now and then. When the hold is nearly full, a load leaves for the fabricators (off the page).

Light

One lighting engine: flames, seams, embers, vats, globes, hot ingots, blows, sparks, lamps and couriers each give off light, laid over the scene in stepped bands. Everything is 1 m blocks: no smooth circles or gradients.

Open questions

  • Joint lines between modules: keep them (shown), or let modules blend into one hull?
  • Should a module's job show in its shape too (forges always keels, stack modules always boxes)?
  • Bigger refineries: more corridors and hubs, or fewer (more modules per sector)?
  • Storage ship bulkheads: keep all five styles, or settle on one or two as the swarm's standard?
